## ANSWER 1
**Target question:** "What happens during an FMCSA New Entrant Safety Audit?"

---

Most carriers don't realize the New Entrant Safety Audit is a **document audit** — not a road inspection. FMCSA doesn't follow your trucks. They sit down with your records.

Here's exactly what they check across 6 domains:

**1. Driver Qualification Files**
Every driver needs a complete DQ file: application, MVR, medical certificate, road test, annual review, prior employer history, and Clearinghouse query. Missing any one of these is a citable violation.

**2. Drug & Alcohol Program**
You need a written policy, enrollment in a random testing consortium, and documentation of pre-employment testing. A lot of carriers skip the written policy because they think enrollment is enough. It isn't.

**3. Hours of Service Records**
ELD records, driver logs, and any HOS violations need to be on file for at least 6 months. If your ELD had a malfunction and you didn't document it, that's a violation.

**4. Vehicle Maintenance**
A written PM schedule (not just oil change receipts) plus signed DVIRs for every trip. The signed part is what most carriers miss.

**5. Insurance & Authority**
Your MCS-90 endorsement and BMC-91 cargo coverage need to be on active file with FMCSA — not just with your broker.

**6. Accident Records**
A written accident register. Not an email thread. Not a folder. A written register with date, location, fatalities/injuries/tows, and whether the driver was cited.

---

**The rating outcomes:**
- **Satisfactory** — you're clear
- **Conditional** — 60 days to correct deficiencies
- **Unsatisfactory** — revocation proceedings begin

The audit happens within your first 18 months. Most carriers get 12–18 months in before they hear anything, which means the clock is already running when they open their doors.

The system isn't hard to build — but you have to actually build it before they show up.

## ANSWER 2
**Target question:** "How do I start a trucking company and stay compliant with FMCSA?"

---

There's a sequencing problem most new carriers run into: they get their authority, buy equipment, hire drivers, and *then* try to figure out compliance. By that point, they're already exposed.

Here's the order that actually works:

**Step 1 — Know your exposure before you spend anything**
Before you buy a packet, hire a consultant, or touch a document, run a quick diagnostic on where you're exposed. FMCSA cares about 6 areas: Driver Qualification, Drug & Alcohol, HOS, Vehicle Maintenance, Insurance, and Accident Records. Most new carriers have gaps in 3–4 of them before Day 1.

**Step 2 — Build the document architecture first**
You need a file system, not just files. A DQ file that's in a random folder on your desktop isn't audit-ready. A Drug & Alcohol policy in a PDF you can't find is the same as not having one. The architecture matters.

**Step 3 — Install domain by domain**
Don't try to do everything at once. Build Driver Qualification first (it's the most complex), then Drug & Alcohol, then HOS, then Maintenance, then Insurance continuity. 90 days is a realistic timeline if you work it systematically.

**Step 4 — Keep records, not intentions**
FMCSA doesn't care what you meant to do. They care what you can show them. Every policy needs to be dated, signed, and accessible. Every driver file needs to be complete before the driver runs.

## ANSWER 3
**Target question:** "What are the most common reasons new trucking companies fail their FMCSA audit?"

---

After working with new carriers through the compliance build process, the failures almost always come from the same patterns. They're not random. They're preventable.

**The top 5 I see most often:**

**1. No written Drug & Alcohol policy**
Carriers join a consortium and think that's the whole D&A requirement. It's not. 49 CFR 382.601 requires a written policy distributed to every driver. No policy = automatic violation.

**2. Incomplete DQ files**
The DQ file has 8 required components. Most carriers have 4 or 5. The missing ones — annual driving record review, prior employer safety performance history, Clearinghouse query — are easy to overlook and easy for FMCSA to catch.

**3. No PM schedule in writing**
"We do oil changes every 10,000 miles" is not a maintenance program. You need a written preventive maintenance schedule with intervals, and DVIR records showing drivers are signing pre/post-trip inspections.

**4. Insurance lapse window**
Even a 1-day gap between policy cancellation and replacement puts your FMCSA filing out of compliance. Carriers often don't know their broker cancelled and re-issued — the FMCSA filing doesn't update automatically.

**5. MCS-150 not biennial filed**
Your operating authority becomes invalid if you miss the biennial update. FMCSA sends reminders — but many carriers miss them because they go to an old email address from when the authority was registered.

---

None of these are hard to fix before the audit. All of them are hard to fix after.

## ANSWER 4
**Target question:** "What is a Driver Qualification file and what goes in it?"

---

A Driver Qualification (DQ) file is the documentation package every regulated motor carrier is required to maintain for each commercial driver. It's what FMCSA checks first in a New Entrant audit.

**The 8 required components (49 CFR Part 391):**

1. **Driver Application** (391.21) — The carrier's own application form, not just a resume. Must ask specific questions about prior driving history, accidents, and violations.

2. **Motor Vehicle Record (MVR)** (391.23) — Pulled from every state the driver held a license in for the past 3 years. Must be pulled *before* the driver operates, and annually after that.

3. **Medical Examiner's Certificate** (391.41–391.45) — Current physical. If it's expired, the driver is disqualified.

4. **Road Test Certificate** (391.31) — Or a CDL copy if the carrier accepts that as the equivalent.

5. **Annual Driving Record Review** (391.25) — A signed review of the MVR every 12 months. Most carriers have the MVR but forget the annual review document.

6. **Driver's License Copy** (391.11) — Current, correct class, no restrictions that conflict with the operation.

7. **Prior Employer Safety Performance History** (391.23) — The carrier must request 3 years of safety data from each prior employer. If prior employers don't respond, the request and non-response must be documented.

8. **Clearinghouse Query Results** (382.701) — FMCSA's Drug & Alcohol Clearinghouse. Pre-employment query required before first operating day.

---

**Common mistakes:**
- File exists but components are incomplete (missing annual review is most common)
- MVR pulled after driver starts operating instead of before
- Clearinghouse query skipped because carrier didn't know about it

## ANSWER 5
**Target question:** "What is the difference between a DOT compliance consultant and doing it yourself?"

---

Here's an honest breakdown based on what I've seen in practice:

**The consultant route:**
- Cost: typically $2,500–$8,000+ for a full compliance build
- You get someone who knows the requirements doing the work
- The risk: you don't know *why* things are built the way they are — so when something expires, changes, or needs updating, you're dependent again
- Good fit for: carriers who are already operating and don't have bandwidth to learn the system

**The DIY route:**
- Cost: a document system bundle runs $499 if you get a structured one
- You build it yourself — which means you understand it when FMCSA shows up
- The risk: you have to actually do it, and do it in the right order
- Good fit for: new carriers in their first 90 days who have time to install it properly

**The honest truth:**
Most of what a compliance consultant charges for is document organization and knowing where to look in the CFR. That knowledge is learnable. FMCSA publishes exactly what they want to see — it's not hidden.

Where people go wrong DIY is skipping the sequence. They build maintenance records before they have a DQ file. They get an ELD before they have an HOS policy. The documents exist but they don't form a system.

The right order: Driver Qualification → Drug & Alcohol → HOS → Vehicle Maintenance → Insurance & Authority.
